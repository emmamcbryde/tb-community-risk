# Worked examples and ranking

All numerical values here are **invented for arithmetic verification, not clinical**.

A prior infection probability .2, assay sensitivity .8 and specificity .9 with
a positive result produce posterior `.16/(.16+.08) = 2/3`. Positivity is therefore
not certainty of infection. Over the example's five-year interval from infection
age tau=1, its cumulative-hazard increment is .04.

For two fictional effects RR2 and RR3:

| Policy | Effective multiplier | Absolute risk |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| highest applicable | 3 | `(2/3)*(1-exp(-.12))` |
| additive excess | 4 | `(2/3)*(1-exp(-.16))` |
| multiplicative | 6 | `(2/3)*(1-exp(-.24))` |

Tests evaluate these independently with 50-digit Decimal exponentials as well
as the library's binary64 segment calculations. Multiplying the risk directly
by the RR gives a different result and is not used.

For missing fictional diabetes and smoking states with explicit independent
probabilities .2 and .3, the highest-RR distribution is RR1:.56, RR2:.14, RR3:.30.
Thus absolute risk is `(2/3)*(.56*(1-exp(-.04)) + .14*(1-exp(-.08)) +
.30*(1-exp(-.12))) = .04443032971028184`, not the risk at the mean RR1.74.
Without these supplied priors central risk is unavailable. Separately prescribed
absent/present states can still produce missing-information scenarios.

The example's highest-RR order is fictional_10, fictional_05, the tie
fictional_02/fictional_04, fictional_08, fictional_07, fictional_01, fictional_09,
fictional_03, fictional_06. The child fictional_11 and prior-TB fictional_12 remain
unavailable and unranked. Ten of twelve records have calculable central risks.
The largest synthetic result is .25414440546257283; this is an invented scenario,
not a clinical estimate. Full results are regenerated by the documented runner.

## Interpretation

Default ranking uses unconditional absolute risk over a common horizon. Equal
scores receive competition ranks (1,2,2,4), ordered by original string ID. An
optional conditional-progression ranking requires an explicit likely-infected
threshold; no threshold is guessed. Expected cases per 1000 is simply
`1000*absolute risk` for 1000 otherwise identical people, not an independent
measurement of population incidence. Mixed horizons are not ranked unless the
caller explicitly selects a shared horizon.

Missing-information interval overlap is flagged, including ties. Missing ranges
are themselves a reason for ranking uncertainty. Ranges describe absolute risk;
conditional ranking does not convert them into conditional-risk confidence
intervals. Distinct adjacent ranks should not imply meaningful differences when
these scenarios overlap. Neither intervals nor point estimates include all
parameter, sampling, transportability or model uncertainty.

Rankings can reproduce biased ascertainment and unequal access to testing.
Completeness is an information measure, not a measure of personal worth or care
priority. Review missingness, source applicability, population calibration,
fairness and clinical context before any future decision use. This model is for
planning and sequencing, not denying care. It must not replace clinician review,
be used to diagnose active TB or provide a basis for withholding investigation.
