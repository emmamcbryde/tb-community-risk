# Pre-2000 backcast policies: synthetic sensitivity

These examples are invented diagnostic histories, not estimates for Australia,
Viet Nam, PRK or any other country. No WHO incidence-to-pressure mapping is
executed. The exact baseline time is 2024.0 for this mathematical example,
not a relabelling of the frozen demographic reference dates.

## Policies and provisional recommendation

| Policy | Assumption and parameters | Identifiability and sensitivities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constant earliest pressure | Hold the independently estimated 2000 pressure backwards to each birth | Most transparent reference when history is absent; not evidence of historical stability. Compare all alternatives and scale/earliest-window uncertainty |
| Bounded earliest trend | Extend an explicitly fitted early log slope backwards; require finite hazard bounds and reject crossings rather than clipping | Earliest slope often weakly informative about long-past exposure. Vary fit window, slope and maximum history; check old-age saturation |
| Independent historical series | Use supplied infection hazards/risks with dates/units and an explicit joining rule | Preferred when reviewed and population-relevant. Historical disease incidence still needs its own mapping; exposure abroad may invalidate national history |
| Anchor-informed parsimonious rise/decline | Fix present scale and post-2000 curve; fit at most one historical slope initially to older age anchors | One extra slope needs independent older-cohort information; freeing scale, slope, age exposure, clearance and assay jointly is not identified |
| Earlier-history uncertainty distribution | Draw a coherent prehistory parameter/curve, not independent annual noise | Report mixture predictions or scenario ranges; priors do not create empirical identification. Declare distribution, support, correlation, RNG and join |

Provisional central reference: explicit constant earliest pressure **only after
the user/reviewer selects it**, when no credible history is available.
There is no automatic backcast in the prototype. A reviewed historical curve
takes priority over a convenient reference. Required sensitivity set:
bounded early decline/rise; credible independently supplied history; one
anchor-informed historical parameter where supported; and broad coherent
prehistory uncertainty. Backcast length must cover the oldest included birth.
No historical mortality or migration is invented to complete coverage.

## Worked construction

For every policy, post-2000 pressure is
lambda(t) = 0.01 exp[-0.02(t-2000)] per susceptible person-year through 2024.
The integrated post-2000 hazard is
0.01(1-exp[-0.02*24])/0.02. Pre-2000 variants are:

1. Constant 0.01/year.
2. Extend that same 2%/year decline backwards, with maximum permitted hazard
   0.1/year; all example ages satisfy the bound. No cap is applied.
3. Supplied synthetic steps: 0.005/year during 1900-1959, 0.020 during
   1960-1979, 0.012 during 1980-1999. The boundary jump is explicit.
4. A 1%/year historical decline joined continuously at 2000. With present
   scale fixed, its synthetic age-65 anchor is 50.213% (unrounded value
   generated by the equation); solving the single historical slope against
   that invented anchor recovers 0.01/year. This is not an empirical fit.
5. Pre-2000 constant multipliers 0.5, 1, 2 with probabilities 0.25, 0.50, 0.25.
   The table gives the weighted **prevalence**, not prevalence computed from
   the mean hazard. Individual draws may jump at 2000; that join is explicit.

Total ever-infection prevalence is 1-exp(-H). For the exponential segment,
integrate the exponential analytically; supplied steps integrate duration
times rate. Sexes would be identical only because these synthetic curves
are specified identically. Competing mortality, migration, clearance,
reinfection and disease exits are excluded as in the diagnostic.

## Results

| Backcast | Age 4 | Age 24 | Age 40 | Age 65 | Age 85 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| constant_earliest | 2.544% | 17.354% | 29.574% | 45.152% | 55.094% |
| bounded_early_trend | 2.544% | 17.354% | 31.557% | 56.214% | 74.948% |
| supplied_history | 2.544% | 17.354% | 31.792% | 56.639% | 60.765% |
| anchor_conditional_slope | 2.544% | 17.354% | 30.519% | 50.213% | 64.336% |
| uncertain_earlier_level | 2.544% | 17.354% | 30.711% | 46.644% | 56.217% |

Ages 4 and 24 require no pre-2000 history and agree across policies. At age 85,
plausible-looking synthetic alternatives span 55.094% to 74.948%; that is
structural sensitivity, not a calibrated confidence interval. The uncertainty
mixture alone spans 39.080%-75.601% at age 85 (unrounded outputs are in CSV).
Recent first-infection prevalence also changes because the remaining
never-infected stock depends on older history, even with identical recent
pressure. A most-recent-event model would behave differently.

Reproduce with:
    python scripts/preview_country_tb_catalytic.py --output abm/output/country_tb_m2b_audit/synthetic_backcasts.csv

The source script uses independent closed-form integrals; tests compare them
with numerical quadrature. The CSV includes ages 0,1,4,10,24,40,65,85, total,
recent-first, remote-only and scenario minimum/maximum. No modelled pressure
is generated for a real country. Missing supplied history or violated bounds
fail explicitly.
