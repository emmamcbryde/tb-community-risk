# Milestone 2B: incidence, infection history and TBI calibration design

This is a scientific design and an isolated synthetic mathematical diagnostic,
not a fitted country model. The frozen 2A data and loader contracts are unchanged.
The model is for planning and sequencing, not for denying care.

Read with the [candidate comparison](mapping_candidates.md), [backcast examples](backcast_synthetic_examples.md),
[contract proposals](calibration_contracts.md), [independent data recheck](frozen_recheck_2b.md)
and [decision register](decision_register_2b.md). The M1
[identifiability note](incidence_to_infection.md) is historical context; the
distinctions and provisional recommendations here govern the next milestone.

## 1. Target, notation and units

Let \(t\) denote continuous calendar time in years, \(a\) exact attained age in
years, \(b=t-a\) birth time, \(s\) source-recorded sex and \(g\) geography.
An exact baseline time \(t_0\), not just its integer year, must be supplied.
The baseline year remains **2024**: ABS stock is 30 June, WPP stock is 1 July.
A midyear convention such as \(t_0=2024.5\) is an approximation to be recorded.
Annual WHO 2024 data describe the whole calendar year; an annual pressure
interval is \([2024,2025)\). Midyear integration uses only its first half.
Using full-year estimates to describe that half is a retrospective assumption,
not a prospective prediction available at midyear.

| Quantity | Definition and units | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(I^{WHO}_g(t)\) | WHO estimated new/relapse active TB disease episodes per 100,000 population-years | A published disease estimate, not a count of infections or raw notifications |
| \(C^{not}_g(t)\) | Reported notifications, cases/year | Requires detection, delay and case-definition model to compare with disease onset |
| \(\lambda_g(t,a,s)\) | Exogenous first-acquisition hazard, infections per susceptible person-year, equivalently year\(^{-1}\) | Neither \(I/100000\) nor a Styblo conversion |
| \(q(t_1,t_2)\) | Conditional probability of acquisition on an interval, \(1-\exp[-\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\lambda\,dt]\), unitless | A probability, not a rate |
| \(H(a,s,g)\) | Lifetime integrated hazard, unitless | Requires history back to birth |
| \(P_E\) | Ever acquired infection by baseline | Equals retained TBI only under explicit no-clearance/no-exit assumptions |
| \(P_L\) | Prevalence of retained/viable infection among living people without active disease under the eventual state definition | Not directly observed by an immune assay |
| \(P_R,P_M\) | Recent and remote-only shares of the baseline population | Define clock, window and eligible states; not progression RRs |
| \(h_D(a,\tau,s,z,t)\) | Disease progression hazard per infected person-year, duration \(\tau\), covariates \(z\) | Age at infection, current age and duration matter |
| \(\sigma(a,\tau,z)\lambda\) | Reinfection hazard in previously infected people, year\(^{-1}\) | Susceptibility \(\sigma\) requires evidence; may reset last-event clock |
| \(\gamma(a,\tau,z)\) | Clearance of retained infection, year\(^{-1}\) | Biological clearance differs from assay reversion |
| \(\rho_{assay}\) | Loss/change of assay positivity under an observation model | Does not prove clearance |
| \(\mu_x(a,s,t)\) | Mortality hazard in infection/disease state \(x\), deaths/person-year | Competing exit; differential survival changes prevalence among survivors |
| \(m_x(t,a,s,g)\) | Migration/entry flows, persons/year per age/sex/state | Entrants can carry infection acquired elsewhere |
| \(\lambda^{dyn}\) | Pressure generated by infectious TB and contact/mixing in a transmission model | Deferred to separate dynamic engine |

Sex strata are retained throughout. Equal initial diagnosis/treatment effects
by sex do not imply equal exposure, smoking, survival or observed TBI. No sex
effect is estimated merely because sex-specific population counts exist.
No adult TSTin3D risk values enter this milestone.

Immune tests have no infection gold standard and measure responses rather than
directly establishing viable infection. That limits the interpretation of
assay-anchored \(P_L\). See [WHO testing guidance](https://tbksp.who.int/en/node/633).
Historical risk reconstruction with demography has precedent in
[Houben and Dodd (2016)](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1002152);
its conversion assumptions and parameter values are not adopted here.

## 2. Explicit disease-to-infection mapping

Describe positive temporal shape \(r(t)\), normalize it on a fixed reference
period \(T_{ref}\), and write

\[
 \lambda(t,a,s,g)=k_g\,r_g(t;\theta)\,w_g(a,s,t),\qquad
 \exp\{\operatorname{mean}_{T_{ref}}\log r\}=1.
\]

\(k_g\) is year\(^{-1}\); \(r,w\) are dimensionless. Fix the normalization
weights/vintage (and the reference population mean of \(w\), fixed at one)
so updating demography cannot silently change the meaning of
\(k\). A candidate descriptor is
\(\log r=\beta\{\log f(I^{WHO})-\overline{\log f(I^{WHO})}\}+\delta(t)\),
where \(f\) is a documented descriptive smoother. This equation is a
**hypothesis about shape**, not a natural-history identity. \(\beta=1\) and
\(\delta=0\) require explicit scenario approval. With \(\beta\), \(k\), \(w\)
and a free discrepancy curve all unconstrained, the inverse problem is not
identified. Do not fit them all from the same 25 annual points.

To claim that incidence constrains pressure scientifically, an explicit forward
operator must generate disease from infection cohorts:

\[
 \widehat C_g(t)=\sum_s\int\!\int\!\sum_z
 L_g(a,s,\tau,z,t)\,h_D(a,\tau,s,z,t)\,d\tau\,da
 + C_{\mathrm{specified\ other\ disease\ pathways}}(t),
 \quad \widehat I_g(t)=10^5\widehat C_g(t)/N_g(t).
\]

\(L\) is the surviving infected stock density (persons per age-year and
infection-duration-year); \(h_D\) has year\(^{-1}\) units. An equivalent
cohort-convolution kernel includes infection flows, progression delay, survival,
clearance and reinfection consistently. First infections enter once; rapid
progression cannot be added both as an infection-flow event and again through
the duration kernel. WHO disease episodes and model disease case definitions
must agree, including relapses and imported disease. Historical denominators,
HIV composition and treatment/detection history cannot be reconstructed from
the frozen 2024 demography alone.

No forward disease operator is implemented in 2B. Until one has reviewed
natural-history inputs and checks, incidence-derived curves are labelled
**candidate pressure-shape scenarios, not disease-calibrated histories**.
Scaling a shape with TBI observations can calibrate cumulative exposure
conditional on that shape; it does not validate the shape against disease.
PRK cannot generate an incidence-informed shape at all without new reviewed
inputs. An independently supplied pressure history is a separate explicit mode.

## 3. Catalytic construction: restricted diagnostic

Follow a person born at \(b=t_0-a\):

\[
 H(a,s,g)=\int_0^a\lambda(t_0-a+u,u,s,g)\,du,\quad
 S(a)=P(\text{never infected})=e^{-H(a)}.
\]

For recent window \(r>0\), split at \(v=\max(0,a-r)\):
\(H_B=\int_0^v\lambda\,du\), \(H_R=\int_v^a\lambda\,du\), \(H=H_B+H_R\).
In the **first-infection-only, absorbing ever-infected diagnostic**:

\[
 U=e^{-H},\quad R_{\mathrm{first}}=e^{-H_B}(1-e^{-H_R}),
 \quad M_{\mathrm{first}}=1-e^{-H_B},\quad T=R_{\mathrm{first}}+M_{\mathrm{first}}=1-U.
\]

All are proportions of the whole age/sex population. \(M_{\mathrm{first}}\)
means first infected before the window, with no allowed later reinfection.
At birth \(a=0\), \(U=1\); for \(a<r\), remote-only is zero. This diagnostic
estimates ever-infection, provisionally called retained TBI only in its
explicit no-clearance/no-disease-exit scenario. It is not a clinical
latent-infection estimator in the presence of active TB, past treatment or
differential survival.

For a *different*, repeat-event Poisson diagnostic with no protection or
clearance, the **most recent event** states are

\[
 U=e^{-H},\quad R_{\mathrm{last}}=1-e^{-H_R},\quad
 M_{\mathrm{last}}=e^{-H_R}-e^{-H}.
\]

Here recent includes remote-infected people re-exposed in the window. It must
not be substituted for \(R_{\mathrm{first}}\). With partial immunity,
clearance, disease or heterogeneous exposure, these repeat-event equations
generally fail and explicit state/duration transitions are needed.

The first-event time density is
\(f(u)=\lambda(b+u,u,s,g)\exp[-\int_0^u\lambda(b+v,v,s,g)dv]\).
Divide by \(1-e^{-H}\) to condition on ever infection. A future synthetic
baseline can sample this density using an explicit RNG and store time since
first infection. Most-recent infection and reinfection counts require another
model. Neither a midpoint of the recent band nor a user-entered recent
prevalence is an acceptable substitute for an infection-history draw.

The module engine/country_tb/catalytic_diagnostic.py implements only piecewise-constant
calendar pressure integration, these first-event probabilities, synthetic
one-scale inversion and categorical sampling. Age exposure \(w=1\) is a
diagnostic restriction, not an approved population assumption. It accepts no
WHO series and imports no projection/model code. Sex/geography/scenario and
provenance are mandatory. Overlapping/gapped histories, missing lifetime
coverage, negative/nonfinite hazards and invalid targets fail without clipping.
All exclusions are recorded on each result:

- reinfection;
- clearance or assay reversion;
- disease progression/treatment exits;
- selective mortality;
- migration.

Probability survivor functions are evaluated at decimal precision 80 and
rounded to 40 decimal places (absolute state error bounded by \(10^{-40}\));
the three stored states and total reconcile algebraically, without clipping
or fitted normalization. Integrals use decimal arithmetic; extreme numerical
range errors fail. The categorical diagnostic uses a caller-supplied RNG and
does not create people, infection times or a common baseline population.

## 4. Observed TBI anchor and calibration

Use immutable survey observations with numerator/denominator (if available),
reported prevalence, uncertainty, exact age interval, sex, geography, survey
date/year, assay/product, threshold and units, sampling frame/weights,
design/covariance, source/vintage, review state and exclusions of active TB or
previous treatment. Preserve inconsistent source summaries; flag and resolve
their rounding/weighting discrepancy before fitting. Do not replace missing
numerators or denominators with invented effective counts.

For survey \(j\), construct age/sex/location/time-specific model probabilities
at **its survey time**, not by copying the 2024 cross-section backwards.
Use the survey population weighting, not the convenience display bands:

\[
 \pi_j(\theta)=
 \sum_{a,s,g}\omega_{j,a,s,g}\,
 {\cal O}_{assay_j,threshold_j}(P_{\mathrm{states}}(a,s,g,t_j;\theta)),
 \qquad \sum\omega_j=1.
\]

A minimal sensitivity/specificity operator is
\(\pi=Se\,P+(1-Sp)(1-P)\). Its parameters must be specific to the assay,
threshold, target state and population, with uncertainty. Persistent immune
response after clearance, reversion, BCG/NTM effects for TST, indeterminate
tests, young children and immunosuppression can require a richer state-linked
operator. IGRA, TST and antigen skin tests are not exchangeable. Identity
observation is permitted only for synthetic validation or a clearly reviewed
assumption, never an unlabelled default for real tests.

Choose an objective explicitly:

\[
 Q(\theta)=D_{TBI}(\pi_j,p_j)+D_I(\widehat I,I^{WHO})+
 P_{\mathrm{prior/regularization}}(\theta).
\]

An independent binomial likelihood is suitable only for an actual simple
sample with compatible counts. Complex survey data require design-based
uncertainty/effective information or covariance; serial/overlapping surveys
are dependent. WHO bounds do not provide a joint likelihood or temporal
covariance. Without a disease operator, omit \(D_I\) and label the calibration
**TBI-anchored conditional pressure scenario**, not incidence-fitted.
Do not double-use the same TBI data as independent priors and likelihood.

Initial reviewed calibration should free **one scale \(k\)**, holding shape,
age exposure, backcast and observation model fixed. Predeclare finite parameter
bounds and tolerances, initial values, optimizer version and convergence
criteria. Later allow one historical slope only when anchors span enough
birth cohorts. Report profile objectives/likelihoods, Jacobian rank and
condition, parameter correlations, boundary hits, predictive residuals by
age/sex, held-out predictions and scenario spread. Optimization success does
not establish identifiability.

| Evidence | Identifies conditionally | Does not identify alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Incidence alone | Disease burden and, with a fixed reviewed disease kernel, some pressure trajectory constraints | Absolute infection scale, progression scale, remote stock, recency, age exposure or migration |
| No TBI anchor | No empirical infection scale in this milestone; accept only an explicit prior/scenario range | A unique TBI estimate or a finite data-derived confidence interval |
| One overall prevalence | One monotone scale if weights, history, assay and other parameters are fixed and informative | Backcast shape, age gradient, recent/remote split or assay accuracy jointly |
| Multiple age-specific prevalences | Cohort cumulative hazards and perhaps a small historical parameter set | Unrestricted age vs period vs cohort effects, exact event timing, clearance |
| Sex-specific observations | Sex exposure contrast conditional on common natural history/assay assumptions | Sex progression or treatment effects merely from different TBI prevalence |
| Serial surveys/conversion cohorts | Additional temporal constraints over observed intervals | Immunological persistence, selective follow-up and reinfection without appropriate models |
| Incidence plus TBI | Can reduce infection/progression tradeoff with a reviewed disease kernel and matched populations | Structural uniqueness with unrestricted progression, detection, migration, HIV and measurement error |

National anchors do not automatically calibrate a subnational pressure scale.
Any transportability rule or hierarchical sharing requires geography,
population comparability, uncertainty and approval. A local incidence scale
factor is not automatically a local infection-pressure factor.

Modes are **Model-estimated TBI profile** and **Observed TBI profile used as
calibration anchor**. A direct user profile override is a separate reviewed,
hashed observation/anchor artifact referencing its parent; it cannot mutate
WHO, demography or prior results. Fit approved parameters against it using its
weights/assay semantics. For a fully specified exact profile, test feasibility
under the restricted curve; a finite positive hazard cannot attain prevalence
1. Under the calendar-only/no-clearance model at a common time, cumulative
hazard is nondecreasing with age; a decreasing exact age profile cannot fit.
This monotonicity need not hold with age exposure, migration or selective
survival. Reject incompatible exact constraints. For noisy observations,
preserve residuals and return incompatible/review_required when declared
fit criteria fail. Do not clamp, force a fit or quietly change the model.

## 5. Survival, demography and the 2024 cross-section

The catalytic \(e^{-H}\) formula describes a cohort acquisition process.
Interpreting it among survivors assumes survival independent of infection
history conditional on modelled strata, no selective disease exits and no
migration selection. It is not a mortality model.

Generally, for history path \(x\), the 2024 survivor distribution is proportional
to \(P(x)\exp[-\int\mu_x(u,s,b+u)du]\). For example, susceptible survivors carry
weight \(\exp[-\int(\lambda+\mu_U)du]\); infected survivors integrate the
first-event density times survival before/after the event. Normalize by all
surviving states, including any explicitly retained disease/recovered states.
Infection-independent mortality cancels within an exact age/sex stratum;
infection-dependent TB mortality does not. Do not apply a second survival
factor to already living ABS/WPP 2024 counts.

The frozen demography supplies living population weights \(N_{a,s,2024}\);
multiply conditional probabilities by these weights to form a synthetic
cross-section. Changing births/cohort sizes influence aggregate age weights,
not each person's acquisition probability. A closed birth cohort projected
forward would additionally need births, deaths and flows, which are outside
this milestone. Historical mortality tables by sex and age and historical
cohort/migration data are not supplied by the frozen count snapshots.

Migration is omitted in the diagnostic, not estimated as zero. High-prevalence
inward migrants into a low-pressure setting can make local lifetime exposure
underestimate older TBI; migration from lower-pressure origins can cause the
opposite error. Selective emigration, infection-associated death and return
migration alter both magnitude and direction. No universal correction sign
is defensible. Australia especially requires origin/residence histories or
explicit migrant scenarios before national pressure backcasts are interpreted
as residents' actual exposures.

Single-year count cells such as age 20 represent \([20,21)\), not an exact
20th birthday. Future integration must average probability over a reviewed
within-cell age/date distribution; a midpoint approximation needs a recorded
convergence check. Preserve fine ages internally and aggregate weighted
infected counts to 0–4, 5–14, 15–44, 45–64, 65+ separately by sex, then combine
counts once. Both-only data remain both-only.

For 100+, the diagnostic cannot use age 100 as a silent representation of all
older people. Require finer external age weights or report an explicit
bounded/scenario tail; absent either, mark tail unestimated and report covered
population, without claiming a complete prevalence. The original open group
remains unchanged. Similar care applies to survey open age intervals.

## 6. Uncertainty and validation boundary

| Source | Required propagation |
| --- | --- |
| WHO intervals | Preserve bounds; use named coherent trajectories/covariance assumptions, not independent annual uniforms or a guessed confidence level |
| Smoothing/trend | Slope/shape parameter uncertainty and smoothing-choice sensitivity; re-estimate with each scenario |
| Disease-to-pressure mapping | Kernel, elasticity, exposure, progression, detection/HIV and discrepancy scenarios; dominant structural uncertainty |
| Pre-2000 | Explicit historical policies and parameter ranges; old-age prevalence and recency reported separately |
| TBI data | Sampling design, uncertainty, dependency, population matching and observation year |
| Assay | Threshold/age-specific measurement uncertainty; persistence/reversion scenarios distinct from biological clearance |
| Mortality | Sex/age table uncertainty plus differential survival scenarios when included |
| Post-2024 | Separate future pressure and demographic scenario assumptions, not a continuation of observed incidence |

Separate **parameter uncertainty** conditional on a model; **stochastic
population variation** conditional on parameters; **scenario uncertainty**
between structural choices; and **data-source revision uncertainty** between
immutable snapshots. Do not pool them into an unexplained interval. Pair
scenario/parameter draws across later engines, preserve RNG algorithm/seed
and draw IDs, and store all hashes. WHO endpoint trajectories are sensitivities,
not a posterior unless a reviewed probability model supports that claim.

Closed-form tests cover zero/constant/step pressure; rising and declining
piecewise histories; fractional young and old ages; absent history; impossible
targets; exact synthetic age-anchor recovery; sex-weighted reconciliation;
canonical hash invariance and sensitivity; and explicit-RNG sampling. The
sampling tolerance is predeclared per state:
\(6\sqrt{p(1-p)/n}+1/n\), with deterministic seed and \(n=100000\).
No stochastic country population or empirical assay fitting is executed.

The eventual calibrated age profile and infection-time representation are
created once and referenced by both static/direct-effects and dynamic models.
Static projection may explicitly use exogenous future pressure; the dynamic
model must calibrate its transmission state to the same baseline and report
any incompatibility. It cannot silently regenerate another initial TBI stock.
No engine interface is wired here.
