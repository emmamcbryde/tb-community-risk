## Objective and equations

The catalytic model converts an explicitly selected historical pressure shape
q into exposure ratealpha×q. Repeated Poisson infection events remain possible.
The most recent event determines recency and resets the central progression
clock; earlier remote exposure remains auditable. Neither reinfection nor
prior-remote-plus-recent adds another person to total TBI.

Let Hr/Hc be cumulative remote/recent exposure hazards. Never infected is
exp[-(Hr+Hc)], recent is1-exp(-Hc), remote-only isexp(-Hc)[1-exp(-Hr)]. Their
sum is1; any TBI isrecent+remote-only. Prior-remote-plus-recent is
[1-exp(-Hr)][1-exp(-Hc)] and is a subset ofrecent. These unchanged2C semantics
do not prove viable bacilli or remove previously treated TB.

The forward operator applies age/time-since-most-recent-infection baseline
hazard h0 to a resolved disease-free population. For the highest applicable
factor multiplierm, conditional interval risk is1-exp[-m×(H0(t2)-H0(t1))].
It never multiplies cumulative probability directly. Death and first disease
remove future first-episode risk. m is provisionally an RR-as-hazard approximation,
not automatically an observed hazard ratio. No new central effect is approved.
Future exogenous exposure, if explicitly supplied, can infect/reinfect; it is
not generated by modelled cases and is not endogenous transmission.

Observed positivity isSe×TBI+(1-Sp)×(1-TBI). It is not synonymous with latent
infection. One scale can be identified only conditional on pressure/backcast,
natural history, assay, risk factors/association, mortality, migration, prior
disease and observation definitions. A good fit cannot identify them separately.

## Empirical findings and country decisions

The licensed snapshot preserves96 source observations from Viet Nam contact
campaigns, US NHANES and Australian premigration child applications. There are
12 prespecified TBI-anchored comparisons and nine contextual adult forward
comparisons. Detailed age errors, ratios and unresolved denominators are in
the [empirical report](milestone3d_empirical.md).

- **Viet Nam:** age-held-out positivity RMSE8.25points(2020) and2.71(2021),
  cross-campaign transport22.14. Different districts/selection matter. Same-year
  WHO incidence is contextual new/recurrent national incidence, not a compatible
  contact-sample first-episode target. No empirical incidence inversion runs.
- **Australia:** preserve premigration applications separately from residents,
  Australian-born, longer-term migrants and clinical groups. Mixed assays,
  child curves, age ambiguity and missing exposure/residence histories prevent
  latent-profile validation from these records. Locally-born, longer-term
  migrant and compatible clinical-cohort inputs remain data-required. National
  incidence cannot reconstruct their TBI histories.
- **Additional setting, US NHANES:** selected for probability sampling and
  published age/nativity assay estimates, not for fit quality. US-born finite
  adult held-out RMSE1.18points; prediction lies inside two reported intervals.
  Strong specificity sensitivity, unmodelled prior treatment and open ages
  prevent a full-population claim. Other nativity groups remain descriptive.

DirectionA fits TBI alone before any incidence comparison. DirectionB can fit
incidence only after the unchanged3C gate, then validates independent TBI.
Empirical directionB is unavailable for both countries. A synthetic known-scale
test verifies the mechanism without claiming empirical success. Source
observations and user-supplied TBI profiles are never changed to force incidence
agreement. Positive assays after treatment/possible clearance remain a
discrepancy with the no-clearance viable-TBI interpretation.

## Uncertainty and invalid configurations

No central scientific acceptance thresholds are chosen. Proposed absolute
errors2/5/10points, RMSE2.5/5/10points, interval inclusion80/90/95% and unresolved
population0/.1/1% need design-specific approval. They are candidate discussion
values, not a permissive default. Published uncertainty, parameter scenarios,
model discrepancy and numerical integration error remain distinct.

Invalid for country inversion: unresolved children; unspecified tail; unreviewed
RR74/50/30/25.3/16 as universal hazards; missing mortality for long horizons;
notifications/prevalence/imported disease without compatible observation models;
unresolved migration, prior-TB/recurrence or denominators; assay mismatch;
reused fit/validation data; absent approved exclusion threshold. Neither a
unique numerical root nor a small empirical residual removes these failures.
Production status stays `unavailable_pending_progression_and_risk_forward_operator`.

## Dynamic-model handoff and next milestone

In high-transmission populations incidence and infection pressure are jointly
generated. Static inversion can supply initialization, diagnostics, comparison
targets or bounded priors, not replace endogenous transmission. A later
Summer/Starsim handoff must retain immutable source/parameter hashes; exact
age/sex/subgroup populations and unresolved counts; latent versus assay profiles;
most-recent-event distributions and reinfection policy; risk definitions,
memberships and RR review; exposure/arrival histories; active-baseline/prior-TB
exclusions; and mortality/recurrence assumptions. Intervention-modified infection
pressure requires the dynamic model. Neither workstream is modified here.

Recommended next milestone: scientific collaborator review and targeted matched
data acquisition, followed by a preregistered external/held-out validation plan
and explicit component decisions. Do not begin production UI or approve country
incidence inversion automatically.
